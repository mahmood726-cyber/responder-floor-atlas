# Responder Floor Atlas — E156 Methods Note

**Primary estimand:** ratio of empirical to canonical minimum important difference (MID) per patient-reported-outcome instrument, computed by back-solving per-arm implied MID from normal-approximation reconstruction of reported responder rates in Cochrane reviews that pool the same outcome both continuously and dichotomously.

Do Cochrane reviewers implicit responder-rate thresholds systematically match or differ from the community-published minimum important differences they cite? We scanned Pairwise70 (595 Cochrane reviews); 51 contained dual-framing outcome pairs and four yielded three or more trials with extractable arm-level continuous and dichotomous data. Per trial we back-solved the implied MID per arm from normal-approximation reconstruction of reported responder rates and pooled across trials using REML and HKSJ. Empirical MIDs exceeded canonical by 1.2- to 3.3-fold across body-weight and SF-36 instruments, derived from 4 studies with arm-level data. Metafor parity held within 1e-6; one of four reviews showed an alpha=0.05 framing flip; all four showed a magnitude flip; 94 tests passed; zero Sentinel blocks. Reviewer-chosen responder cutoffs drift upward from guideline MIDs, meaning published responder analyses apply systematically stricter thresholds than their cited literature implies. Four-review evidence is proof-of-concept only; just 8.6% of Cochrane reviews support dual-framing reconstruction, limiting generalizability of the MID-drift estimate.
