# Responder Floor Atlas — E156 Methods Note

**Primary estimand:** ratio of empirical to canonical minimum important difference (MID) per patient-reported-outcome instrument, computed by back-solving per-arm implied MID from normal-approximation reconstruction of reported responder rates in Cochrane reviews that pool the same outcome both continuously and dichotomously.

Do Cochrane reviewers implicit responder-rate thresholds systematically match or differ from the community-published minimum important differences they cite? We scanned Pairwise70 (595 Cochrane reviews); 51 contained dual-framing outcome pairs and four yielded three or more trials with extractable arm-level continuous and dichotomous data. Per trial we back-solved the implied MID per arm from normal-approximation reconstruction of reported responder rates and pooled across trials using REML and HKSJ. Empirical MIDs exceeded canonical by 1.28- to 3.81-fold across body-weight, BMI, and SF-36 instruments (bootstrapped median 95% CI; 2013 observations). Metafor parity held within 1e-6; one framing flip in four reviews; all four showed magnitude flip; log-normal skew bounded p-hat error at 5 pp (p95); truncated-normal at 1 pp. Reviewer-chosen responder cutoffs drift upward from guideline MIDs, meaning published responder analyses apply systematically stricter thresholds than their cited literature implies. Four-review evidence is proof-of-concept only; just 8.6% of Cochrane reviews support dual-framing reconstruction, limiting generalizability of the MID-drift estimate.
